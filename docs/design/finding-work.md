Status: built. Contracts: `FindingTreatmentSchema` and `FindingTreatmentCommandSchema` in `packages/contracts/src/findings.ts`, the action and closure shapes in `packages/contracts/src/rule-evaluations.ts`, the list and the home in `packages/contracts/src/open-findings.ts`. Code: `packages/domain/src/finding-work.ts` (treatments), `rule-evaluations.ts` (closing by a record), `open-findings.ts` (the reads). Migration `0084_finding_work.sql`.

## What a flight finding supported before

A finding the generic evaluator opened about a flight could be taken (`acknowledge`), answered (`respond`) and resolved by its owner once its check had cleared (`resolve`), through the flight's own decision command (`decision.record` with `findingTreatment`), which pins the flight's revision and input cut. A hazard could be opened from it, and a finding packet issued. Nothing closed a finding by itself: a passing evaluation left it open for the owner to resolve. A finding about a person, an aircraft or the organization had the same rows (`findings`, `finding_views`, `finding_treatments`) but no command reached them: the treatment check refused any finding that was not the flight's.

## One model

- **Commands.** The treatment check and the recording (`validateFindingTreatment`, `recordFindingTreatment`) are now about any finding. A flight's decision command still calls them, limited to that flight's findings and pinning the flight's basis. `finding.treat` calls the same two functions for a finding about any subject, flights included, and pins the finding's latest evaluation: a treatment against an older one is refused (`stale_finding`). For a Ledger subject, the module that owns its kind locks its record first (`SubjectFindingView.lockSubject`), then the finding, then the finding's stream, as `lock-order.md` lists for record commands. The event is appended to `finding:<id>` and recorded in `command_records`; a flight's own hook then moves the flight's revision so its screens read it again.
- **What a person records.** `acknowledge` (take it), `assign` (to an active person of the organization holding `decision:write`; anyone who records decisions may assign), `plan` (an action and its due day, a calendar date in the organization's time zone), `respond` (what was done; it marks the action done), `close` (a reason from `corrected_by_record`, `accepted`, `condition_ended`, `recorded_in_error`, and a note). `condition_ended` is for a finding whose condition is over while what it reported still happened: the wind dropped, the aircraft came back inside its area, the late report arrived. Only the owner records `plan`, `respond` and `close`, as only the owner responded before. `resolve` stays for findings of the retired flight evaluator.
- **A person's decision.** Every treatment is refused with `person_required` from a principal that is not a person, whichever command carries it.
- **Closing by a record.** A later result that no longer needs attention means one of two things. A person supplied or corrected a record (the training record arrived, the maintenance entry was completed): the finding is answered by that record. Or the world moved on: what the finding reported still happened. So a finding closes by itself only when its passing evaluation (`pass`, naming no issue) read at least one input the opening evaluation did not, and every such new input is of an observation type marked as a record people keep (`keptRecord` on the type's definition in the registry). It then becomes `resolved` with `closed_by = record`, keeps the closing evaluation (`closing_rule_evaluation_id`), and reads name those records. Otherwise it stays open: the list and the finding say "The latest result is supported by the record; a person closes this finding", and the owner closes it with a reason. A result that no longer applies (`not_applicable`) closes nothing. A later evaluation that needs attention ends that state, and reopens a closed finding as before, comparing issues with the closing evaluation and clearing the closure.
- **Records people keep.** Marked: `operator.record` (every record the organization imports or enters), `organization.record-in-force` (Ledger's own statement of the organization's records in force, made from a person's decisions) and `crew.duty-period` (the organization's roster entries, which people correct and replace). Not marked, because a source reports them about the world: `flight.report`, `position.fix`, `weather.wind`, `battery.state`, `link.status`, `traffic.alert`, `aircraft.fault`, `airspace.authorization` (a service's answer to a request). A new type is unmarked unless its definition says otherwise.
- **Projection.** `finding_views` holds the current action (`action_note`, `action_due_on`, `action_done_at`) and closure (`closed_by`, `closed_at`, `closing_rule_evaluation_id`). Their history is the treatments and the evaluations, which are append-only.

## The list of open findings

`GET /v1/organization/open-findings`: every open finding the generic evaluator opened in the organization, at most 2,000 read, oldest first, ties by id, a page at a time. `shown=counted` (the default) lists findings of active adoptions not relieved by a waiver; `shown=proposed` lists those of shadow adoptions, which are never counted. Each says whether its latest result still needs attention (`cleared`). Filters by subject kind, pack, owner (`none` for nobody) and latest result (`attention`, `cleared`). It also counts every tab, what needs attention (counted findings nobody owns; counted actions past their due day with nothing done), and the values the tab holds to filter by. The ordering and filters are one pure function, `selectOpenFindings`, tested in `tests/unit/open-findings.test.ts`.

The Safety home's attention list adds `findingsWithoutOwner` for people who read evidence.

## The home without an order source

`GET /v1/organization/flights-on-record` says whether the organization has an order source on record: an active source principal holding `flight:create`, or an order or flight intent a source sent. The web app decides by this alone. Without one, it opens on the flights its current flight plan records give (source `operator-records`), upcoming (windows starting now or later, soonest first) and recent (latest first), 25 of each, with the aircraft each plan names, the person a current role assignment names as pilot in command, and the counted open findings about it; and the same attention counts. With one, nothing changes.

## Findings by rule, and one decision for many

An organization that imports months of history and adopts its texts reads hundreds of open findings at once, most of them a few rules repeated for every flight ("the log holds no preflight assessment"), with the few that matter first (a pilot flying on expired training, a flight above its height) among them. Working them one at a time means one closure per flight for one honest answer ("our log did not record that item before November"). Two things answer this: a read grouped by rule, and one decision a person makes about every open finding of one rule.

Code: `groupOpenFindings` and the set read in `packages/domain/src/open-findings.ts`, the decision in `packages/domain/src/finding-sets.ts`. Contracts in `packages/contracts/src/open-findings.ts`. Migration `0088_finding_decisions.sql`.

### The day a finding is about

A finding is about its subject, and its subject's records say when that was: a flight's plan gives its window, a training record the day it was completed. The day a finding is about is the earliest time (an interval's start, else the time it occurred) of the current records about its subject, neither replaced nor withdrawn; a finding whose subject has no record on file is about the time it opened. Every finding in the list carries it (`aboutAt`). The list, the groups and the decision narrow by it with `aboutFrom` and `aboutUntil`, calendar days in the organization's time zone, both included. This names no subject kind: it is the same read for a flight, a person or the organization.

### The grouped read

`GET /v1/organization/open-findings/groups` reads the same findings as the list (the same tab, the same filters by subject kind, pack, owner, latest result and the day they are about) and returns one group per pack and rule: the pack and its title, the rule and its title, the paragraph it checks, how many findings are open, how many distinct subjects they are about, the oldest opening, how many are owned, how many are supported by the record now, and the earliest and latest day they are about; with how many findings and rules that makes, the tab counts and what needs attention. Groups are ordered by how many are open, fewest first by default so the rare findings lead (`order=most` reverses it), then by pack title and rule id. The arithmetic is one pure function, `groupOpenFindings`, tested in `tests/unit/open-findings.test.ts`.

Packs carry no weight on a rule today, and none is invented here: a rule's weight would be a judgement the pack author makes about the text, which the order of the groups would then present as Ledger's. Counting is a fact about the records.

The flat list stays. It gains a filter by rule (`ruleId`), the day filters, and with each finding its latest evaluation's id, which is what a decision on it pins; `matching` says how many findings the filters select across every page.

### One decision for many findings

`finding.treat-many` is one decision of one person about every counted open finding of one rule, optionally narrowed by subject kind and by the days the findings are about:

- **What it records.** One of `acknowledge` (take every one), `assign` (to one person, as for one finding), `plan` (one action and due day on every one) or `close` (one reason from the four, and one note). `respond` and `resolve` stay one finding at a time: what was done is said of a finding, not of a rule.
- **What it names.** The command carries the selection (pack, rule, subject kind, the days) and every finding the person was shown, each with the evaluation it was shown with, and how many that was (`shownCount`, which must equal the number named). It is accepted as one journal event on the stream `findings:<pack>:<rule>` and one command record, which keep the selection, each finding and the count. Each finding gets one treatment row, as a single treatment would, naming the decision it came from (`finding_treatments.decision_id`); a finding's history shows "one decision for 60 findings" beside the person, the reason and the note. A treatment of one finding names its own command, as before.
- **Its basis.** At acceptance the selection is read again from the findings themselves, at most 501 rows, with every finding locked. The command is refused with `stale_finding_set` (409) unless that set is exactly the set named, each finding at the evaluation it was shown with: a finding opened for a 61st flight, one closed by a record, or one evaluated again between display and commit refuses it, as every decision whose basis moved is refused. Nothing is recorded; the person reads the set again.
- **The bound.** One decision covers at most 500 findings. A larger set is refused (`finding_set_too_large`, 400) with a sentence saying to narrow it by the days the findings are about or by subject kind. 500 is the most one screen and one terminal show legibly, and one transaction locks comfortably.
- **Who may.** A person's decision, as for one finding: a credential of a service or a source is refused with `person_required`, whichever command carries it. `plan` and `close` need the person to own every finding in the set; otherwise the command is refused (`finding_owner_required`) saying how many they own, and that one `acknowledge` of the same set takes them all.
- **What it means.** A closure of many says what a closure of one says, finding by finding: the reason, the note, the person, the latest evaluation. Nothing is suppressed. A rule that still finds the same thing for a later flight opens a new finding about that flight; there is no switch to ignore a rule. An organization that wants a rule not to count adopts differently (in shadow, or a pack version without it), which is its own decision.

### Locks

`finding.treat-many` takes, in order: the record of each Ledger subject in the set through its kind's `lockSubject` (for a flight, its view, group 3), kind by kind and in id order within a kind; then the set's `finding_views` rows in one statement in id order (group 6); then the stream `findings:<pack>:<rule>` (group 6). It reads the set once without locks to know which subjects to lock, and again under the locks to compare it with the command. That is the order `finding.treat` takes for one finding, taken for many, and the order the evaluator takes (subject before finding). A subject that joins the set between the two reads (a 61st flight) is not locked, and refuses the command, since the set it reads no longer matches.

## Not done here

Findings from the retired flight evaluator (`flight_evaluations`) are not in the list; nothing records them any more. Recording what was done (`respond`) is one finding at a time.
