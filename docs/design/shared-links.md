# Readers and links to produced records

Status: built. An inspector or an auditor can look at an organization's records in two ways: signed in as a reader, or through a link to one produced set of records that needs no sign-in. Paths are relative to the checkout.

## A reader is a preset over grants

The People screen offers **Reader**: `evidence:read` and `document:read`, with no grant to keep records. A reader opens records and each subject's file, the rules and their coverage, findings, produced records and the links made to them, and the controlled documents. It is not a new kind of principal: an administrator can tick more or less, as with any preset (`packages/contracts/src/people.ts`).

Three things had to change for the preset to mean what it says:

- **A principal who holds only reads changes nothing.** `readCapabilityValues` in `packages/contracts/src/index.ts` lists the capabilities that only read (`flight:read`, `order:read`, `evidence:read`, `document:read`, `sms:read`, `sms:report:read`, `sms:restricted:read`). A principal who holds at least one of them and nothing else is refused every request that is not a `GET` or `HEAD`, with `403 reads_only`, in the API's sign-in hook before the body is read (`apps/api/src/index.ts`), except the four routes of `OPEN_TO_READERS`: reporting a safety concern; a person's own choice of which notices reach them, which changes no record; a read sent as a `POST`, a mission's route suggestion, which needs `flight:read` of its own; and `/v1/commands`, of which such a principal may send only an acknowledgement of a document or a briefing issued to them (checked once the body is read, before it is validated). People who only read already did those: a pilot with `sms:read` alone acknowledges the documents issued to them, and a person with `flight:read` alone reports a concern. This was needed because `evidence:read` alone allows writes: producing records, issuing a finding or decision packet, exporting. Considered instead: a new capability for producing, added to every preset and every person who holds `evidence:read` today. That is a larger change for every organization, and a write route added later with only `evidence:read` would reach a reader again. The rule holds for any route, including ones not written yet. A person with no capability at all is not reading only either. Reporting a safety concern stays open to a reader: Ledger takes a report from anyone, signed in or through a reporting link that needs no sign-in, so refusing it to a signed-in reader would keep nothing out; the report is written by the reporting channel, and a reader cannot read it back.
- **`document:read` opens the controlled documents and no other safety record.** `sms:read` opens every safety record kind, the safety report register among them, so a reader could not hold it. `document:read` opens the records of kind `document` and `risk_matrix`, their ordinary attachments, and a Safety home with nothing in it but the program (`requireSmsRead` in `packages/domain/src/sms-envelope.ts`). The hazard register, the safety reports, the controls and the safety assurance records stay behind `sms:read`; hazards and safety assurance records have no read of their own, so the preset holds none of them, and an administrator who wants a reader to see them ticks `sms:read`, which opens the safety report register too (never a report's content, which needs `sms:report:read`). A document's links still name the records it links to, by their reference and title, as they do for anyone who reads the document.
- **The rules are read with `evidence:read`.** The rule packs, their versions and sources, and the organization's coverage were behind `flight:read` alone, while the Rules section was offered with `evidence:read`. They now take either.

The screens hide every action a person who only reads cannot take: producing records, importing and entering them, issuing a finding packet, exporting, every action of a safety record and recording a document. **Report a safety concern** stays in the bar. A reader opens on **Records**.

`tests/integration/reader-access.integration.test.ts` signs in as a reader and walks every route the API registers, read from the route table through `createApp({ onRoute })`. Every route that is not a read and not in `OPEN_TO_READERS` must answer `403 reads_only`; the test pins that list, refuses every command but an acknowledgement, and submits a report as the reader. Every read is in one of three lists: what a reader is for, asked with real ids and expected to answer; what a reader's grants also open, asked with made-up ids and expected not to be refused; and the rest, expected to be refused. A route added later is in the last list until someone moves it, so the test fails when a reader can reach a new route.

## A link to one produced set

Whoever may produce records makes a link on the produced set's own screen (**Records**, **Produced records**, the set). The link opens that set, and nothing else, to someone who does not sign in.

- **The secret.** 32 random bytes, written `ledger-records-` and base64url. It is returned once; `record_production_links` keeps only its SHA-256, which is unique and is how a link is found. The API's role cannot read the hash back (column grants in `packages/persistence/migrations/0091_record_production_links.sql`).
- **The address.** `/shared#<secret>` on the web app. A browser does not send what follows `#` to a server, so the secret is in no request line, no proxy log and no `Referer`. The page sends it in the body of `POST /v1/shared-records` (the set), `POST /v1/shared-records/file` (one file, by its path) and `POST /v1/shared-records/archive` (the whole .zip). The request log keeps only the method and path of these, as of the safety report routes.
- **What it opens.** The organization's name, the set's reason and who asked, when it was produced and by whom, its record count and both digests, its files as its `manifest.json` lists them with `manifest.json` itself, and the whole .zip. A file is read from the kept .zip after the .zip is checked against its recorded SHA-256, and checked against the SHA-256 its manifest lists. A path not in the set's manifest is not found; there is no production id in any request, so a link cannot name another set. Considered: a page that lists the files and links each by an address. That puts the secret, or a second secret per file, in addresses again.
- **Who opens it.** No one is named. Each opening is kept in `record_production_link_openings`, append-only: when, and whether it was the page, a file (with its path) or the .zip. Ledger keeps no network address, browser or account with it: an opening is a fact about the link, and Ledger keeps as little about a person as the record needs. A proxy in front of Ledger logs addresses on its own; that is the deployment's to configure.
- **Expiry and revocation.** A link expires seven days after it is made unless its maker chooses from one to ninety days; the table refuses more than ninety. Revoking it is at once and once, with the person and the reason. Making one is recorded with who made it and the note that says who it is for. Both are journal events on the stream `record-production-link:<id>`.
- **One answer.** An unknown, expired or revoked secret answers `404 record_link_not_in_use` with the same message, whichever route asked. Nothing in the answer depends on whether an organization or a set exists. The lookup is `ledger_record_production_link`, a security-definer function that reads across organizations by the hash alone and returns only the set; the opening is kept by `ledger_record_production_link_opened`, which checks the link again, so a link revoked between the two calls keeps no opening and answers the same 404.
- **Headers.** The page and every answer of these routes, refusals included, carry `Cache-Control: no-store`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`. A file is sent as an attachment with `nosniff` and a content security policy that runs nothing. The page reads a printed index in a sandboxed frame that runs no script, text as text, and a PDF or a picture in a tab of its own made from the page's memory.

`tests/integration/record-production-links.integration.test.ts` makes, opens, lists and revokes links; checks that only the hash is kept, that the openings name no one, that unknown, expired and revoked links answer alike, and that a link opens only its own set's files and nothing of another organization.

### Restricted content

A produced set cannot hold restricted content. Producing records reads operator records (`operator.record` observations) and, when asked, the ordinary original files they came from (`artifacts`); it never reads safety records, and restricted files are kept apart in `restricted_artifacts`, which it does not read (`packages/evidence/src/record-productions.ts`). Every set says so in its manifest. So any set may be shared by link, and none needs to be refused. A change that let a production include safety records or restricted files would have to refuse a link to such a set.

### Lock order

Making a link takes the journal stream (group 6) and inserts a row no other transaction can see. Revoking takes the link's row `FOR UPDATE`, then the stream. Keeping an opening inserts one row, whose foreign key takes the link's row `FOR KEY SHARE`, and nothing else; it waits for a revocation holding the row and then finds the link revoked. See [lock-order.md](lock-order.md).
